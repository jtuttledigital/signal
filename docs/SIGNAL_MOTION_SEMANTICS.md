# SIGNAL — Motion Semantics

**Purpose:** Define SIGNAL's cohesive visual personality while keeping each behavior distinct and meaningful.

## Core thesis

SIGNAL is one continuous behavioral language, not a collection of separate animations.

Every behavior should preserve the same visual identity while changing geometry, energy, rhythm, convergence, and stability to communicate a different cognitive action.

> SIGNAL is quietly aware, deeply responsive, and mathematically expressive.

## Three-layer model

### Identity layer

Always present across all behaviors:

- one continuous mathematical trace
- restrained cyan identity
- dark spatial field
- phosphor-like persistence
- smooth parametric movement
- coherent transitions
- calm pacing
- visible continuity from the prior behavior

### Behavior layer

The current semantic action:

- Presence
- Attention
- Listening
- Reasoning
- Responding
- Completion

### Modulation layer

Short-lived qualities that modify a behavior without becoming new public states:

- confidence
- uncertainty
- curiosity
- focus
- calm
- novelty
- residual motion memory

## Cohesion rules

1. **One species**  
   All behaviors share line character, persistence logic, transition physics, spatial center, and related frequency families.

2. **Continuity over replacement**  
   Preserve phase, momentum, and residual traces. Do not snap, clear, or swap geometries.

3. **Meaningful distinction**  
   Do not differentiate behaviors only with amplitude, speed, glow, or color. Use symmetry, convergence, openness, attractor stability, directional bias, and rhythm.

4. **Motion memory**  
   Each active behavior may leave a restrained fingerprint that fades naturally into the next.

5. **Progress without demanding attention**  
   Motion should remain informative, calm, and non-intrusive.

## Behavior profiles

### Presence

- **Meaning:** Calm availability
- **Geometry:** Simple orbital or elliptical equilibrium with slight asymmetry
- **Energy:** Low
- **Rhythm:** Slow breath and subtle phase drift
- **Impression:** Quiet awareness
- **Avoid:** A dead static ellipse or generic sine wave

### Attention

- **Meaning:** The system notices intent
- **Geometry:** Contracted, focused orbit with central gathering
- **Energy:** Low to medium
- **Rhythm:** Quick activation and short settling
- **Impression:** Readiness
- **Avoid:** Merely increasing amplitude or chasing the cursor

### Listening

- **Meaning:** Receiving and organizing input
- **Geometry:** Open asymmetric loop with aligned harmonics
- **Energy:** Medium
- **Rhythm:** Responsive resonance with a calmer settled variant after pauses
- **Impression:** Absorption and synchronization
- **Avoid:** Audio-equalizer motion, jitter, or distortion

### Reasoning

- **Meaning:** Exploring, testing, and forming connections
- **Geometry:** Layered folds with orbital, figure-eight, and fold attractors
- **Energy:** Medium-high to high
- **Rhythm:** Discover → hold → transform
- **Impression:** Active cognition and curiosity
- **Avoid:** Constant busyness, random complexity, or a glowing knot

### Responding

- **Meaning:** Thought becomes expression
- **Geometry:** Ordered orbital or directional fold with fewer competing loops
- **Energy:** Medium
- **Rhythm:** Steady and deliberate
- **Impression:** Articulation
- **Avoid:** Looking as exploratory as Reasoning or becoming visually inactive

### Completion

- **Meaning:** Work is resolved and available
- **Geometry:** One clean stable attractor
- **Energy:** Descending
- **Rhythm:** Lock → hold → exhale
- **Impression:** Clarity and resolution
- **Avoid:** Abrupt amplitude reduction or a generic success animation

## Transition grammar

- **Presence → Attention:** Contract and align
- **Attention → Listening:** Open and resonate
- **Listening → Reasoning:** Deepen and begin attractor formation
- **Reasoning → Responding:** Reduce competing forms and establish direction
- **Responding → Completion:** Stabilize and lock
- **Completion → Presence:** Exhale while retaining faint resolved memory
- **Interruption:** Redirect momentum without resetting the signal

## Refinement sequence

### Pass 1 — Semantic audit

Codex reviews the current implementation against this document without changing code.

### Pass 2 — Identity constants

Lock shared characteristics:

- trace hierarchy
- persistence curve
- field contour treatment
- line-weight range
- spatial scale
- sampling density
- transition damping family

### Pass 3 — Behavior separation

Tune all six behaviors so they differ through geometry, rhythm, stability, and convergence—not merely amplitude or speed.

### Pass 4 — Transition choreography

Tune each transition independently, including direct jumps and interrupted sequences.

### Pass 5 — Motion memory

Prototype restrained inherited phase, decaying harmonic influence, and Completion afterglow.

### Pass 6 — Modulation prototype

Prototype one semantic dimension only: **confidence**.

### Pass 7 — Signal Lab controls

Expand `/system` only after the semantic behavior model is working.

### Pass 8 — Visual polish

Compare implementation with concept direction and refine scale, persistence, crossings, holds, field visibility, and negative space.

## Review rubric

Score each behavior from 1–5:

- Cohesion
- Distinction
- Meaning
- Restraint
- Continuity
- Memorability

A behavior is not approved until it scores at least 4 in the first five categories.

## Definition of success

The refinement phase succeeds when:

- behaviors are recognizable without labels
- transitions feel like one living entity changing intent
- Reasoning and Responding communicate different cognitive moments
- Completion feels resolved and earned
- Presence retains subtle memory of prior activity
- motion remains calm and non-intrusive
- the signal feels mathematical but not mechanical
- SIGNAL is recognizable from motion alone
